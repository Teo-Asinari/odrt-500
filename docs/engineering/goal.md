Complete the following work:

1. Establish measurable requirements for size, mass, stroke, force, speed, duty cycle, controllability and mounting. Clearly distinguish owner decisions from provisional assumptions.
2. Evaluate compact drivetrain and cable-routing architectures. Address reflected inertia, winding geometry, rope fatigue, release energy and mechanical load paths. Revise or reject architectures that fail.
3. Select credible component candidates using documented specifications. Track uncertain motor constants, missing ratings, supplier availability and unpriced items explicitly.
4. Build a coupled simulation covering drivetrain mechanics, cable behavior, closed-loop control, supply limits, regeneration, buffer capacitance and ESR, and thermal behavior. Include two-unit bar interaction, asymmetric loading and faults.
5. Develop mechanically consistent CAD and a preliminary electrical schematic, component placement and harness definition. Distinguish engineered details from illustrative geometry.
6. Verify numerical convergence, power and energy accounting, sensitivity to uncertain parameters, and defined normal-operation and fault criteria. Passing software tests alone must not establish physical feasibility.
7. Produce complete preliminary per-unit and paired-system cost estimates, including mounts and the common board.
8. Document the measurements, specialist reviews and controlled validation needed before advancing to hardware.

Finish with a reproducible engineering package and an evidence-backed GO, REVISE or NO-GO assessment. State the supported operating envelope, remaining uncertainties, failed requirements and next validation steps.
